The Story

One Halloween night, a hungry skeleton smells fresh-baked chocolate chip cookies cooling on a witch's windowsill.

A mummy slides under the door. A vampire becomes a bat and flies down the chimney. A ghost floats right through the wall. The skeleton has no bandages to unravel, no wings to grow, and no way through closed doors…

So he tries something none of them thought of. He walks up to the door. He knocks. He asks politely.

A gentle bedtime story for ages 3–8 about kindness, courage, and the power of simply asking.

Before it was a book, it was a bedtime story

Andrew Scott made this story up out loud, at bedtime, for his daughter Vitalina Rose. The hungry skeleton, the mummy, the vampire, the ghost — he invented them one night and gave each of them a voice. Then he told it again. And again, the way you do when a child asks for the same story every night.

It was a performance long before it was a manuscript, which is the reason it reads aloud the way it does. Every line in this book has been tested on the only audience that never pretends to be interested.

When it was finally written down, Vitalina’s grandmother, V. Khromatidi, painted it — every watercolor spread by hand, each one bordered by a golden rope she painted herself.

A father who made it up, a grandmother who painted it, and the little girl it’s dedicated to. Three generations of one family made this book.
